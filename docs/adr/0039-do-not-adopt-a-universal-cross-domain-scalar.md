# Do not adopt a universal cross-domain scalar

SVG Diff will not define one scalar that claims geometry displacement, painted area, color change, raster error, presence, resource changes, and future perceptual measurements are naturally commensurate. Named raw magnitude fields remain authoritative, and `v1_domain_lexicographic` continues to order only differences from the exact same domain.

A future versioned Impact Assessment may still identify main Visual Events across domains. Its first design should be a structured policy result: retain the raw evidence vector, expose the rules and thresholds that fired, permit ties or incomparability, and link every conclusion to its contributing events and differences. It may use calibrated tiers or a deterministic decision procedure without pretending the output is a universal physical distance.

The current human tiers and corpus categories are evaluation labels. They cannot become engine weights, thresholds, or severity claims until a separate calibration item records the representative corpus, independent review procedure, agreement, policy inputs, profile, and metric versions.

Reopen scalar adoption only when a concrete text-only Agent task demonstrably requires a total cross-domain order that a structured assessment with ties cannot solve. Any proposed scalar must then record an opaque policy ID, every input and missing-value rule, normalization, weights, thresholds, tie-breaking, calibration corpus, metric versions, sensitivity analysis, and failures against the raw-vector baseline. Adoption would be an additive policy output and could never replace measurements, establish equality, or create and erase Atomic Differences.

This decision implements the direction accepted in ADR 0007 and ADR 0008. It does not complete the separate Impact Assessment definition or threshold-calibration roadmap items.
